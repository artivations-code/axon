For Birth & First Parents

Independent support for birth mothers, birth fathers, and first parents — not tied to any agency, not limited to placement, not temporary.

Not everyone who placed a child uses the same language for themselves. Birth mother, birth father, first parent, biological parent — people hold different words, different contexts, and different levels of choice in what happened. All of it belongs here.

Indiana birth parent support is almost entirely agency-linked and placement-adjacent. Support ends when the paperwork does. Beyond the Sun is different: we are a private, independent practice with no agency ties and no agenda around placement decisions. We serve birth parents at any point — whether placement happened last year or thirty years ago, whether contact exists or does not, whether the feelings are grief, relief, anger, complicated love, or all of them at once.

Birth Mothers

The grief of placing a child does not resolve on a fixed timeline. Anniversaries arrive. Due dates land. Reunion brings its own layered weight. And for many birth mothers, there has never been a space to say all of it without someone else’s comfort taking priority. This is that space.

Birth Fathers

Birth fathers are the most consistently unnamed members of the adoption constellation. Adoption records often contain little from their perspective. Not all birth fathers were given input into the placement decision. And almost no Indiana practice names them publicly or holds space for their specific experience. We do.

Birth fathers carry grief, regret, anger, powerlessness, and questions about a child they may never know — or about a grown adult who is now searching for them. That deserves real, specific support, not a footnote.

What support can look like

  • Processing placement grief at any stage, including decades later
  • Preparing for reunion or first contact
  • Navigating the nervous system weight of anniversaries, “wondering” time, and unknown outcomes
  • Building language for experiences that are rarely witnessed by others
  • Navigating open adoption complexity and contact that has changed over time
  • Support for birth parents whose child has initiated search

This is independent care — not a referral, not a hotline, not a group you found because the agency suggested it. It is clinical-depth support from a practice that has lived experience on the team and understands adoption from the inside.

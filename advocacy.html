Advocacy · Art therapy in Indiana

Indiana does not license art therapists. We’re working to change that.

IndiATA advocates for independent state licensure of art therapists — clear standards for education and supervised practice, protection of the title “art therapist,” and Medicaid access for the Hoosiers art therapists serve.

Standards

Clear qualifications

A license defines who may practice, anchored to nationally recognized graduate education and supervised-hours standards.

Access

Medicaid access

Recognition as approved mental health providers opens Medicaid reimbursement, so more Hoosiers can access art therapy.

Protection

Title protection

Only qualified, credentialed practitioners may advertise themselves as art therapists.

Nationally

A number of states already license art therapists, and more introduce legislation each session. AATA keeps the current state-by-state picture on its State Advocacy page.

Before you write

I’m not an art therapist. Can I still speak up?

Yes. Legislators weigh messages from anyone who lives in their district. Clients, families, educators, employers, and colleagues in other professions all have standing to say why access to qualified art therapists matters to them.

What makes a message effective?

Three short parts: where you live, what you are asking for, and one specific reason it matters to you. A few sentences in your own words carries further than a long letter.

Should I mention candidates or parties?

Keep your message about the policy. This work is about licensure standards, not elections — naming candidates or parties moves the conversation away from the ask.

Can I share my own experience of art therapy?

You decide what to share and how much. A general statement about what access meant to you is enough — no diagnosis, treatment detail, or private health information is needed, and nothing you share with a legislator is confidential.

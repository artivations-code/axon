For Adoptees

Adoption-real, nervous-system-aware support for people living the after of adoption at any age.

Adoption leaves fingerprints on identity, belonging, attachment, and the nervous system — even when the story on paper looks fine. Many adopted people live highly functional lives and still carry questions, patterns, or a persistent internal pull that has never had language.

We work with adoptees across the full lifespan: children, adolescents, adults, and people in midlife or later who are revisiting adoption for the first time or the hundredth. Sessions can include talk, art, pattern mapping, somatic tracking, or building language for things that have never had words.

We specifically support

  • Identity, belonging, and the question of “who am I” across developmental stages
  • Nervous system patterns that trace back to early separation, even in pre-verbal experience
  • Adoption grief — including grief that is ambiguous, disenfranchised, or unnamed
  • Reunion and contact navigation: before, during, and in the long aftermath
  • Loyalty pulls and the relational complexity of living between two families
  • High-functioning adoptees who look okay on the outside and are tracking something real underneath
  • Transracial and transcultural adoption identity

Late-discovery adoptees

Finding out about your adoption in adulthood — whether at 20, 40, or 70 — carries its own shape of disruption. The identity reorientation, the sense of betrayal, the questions about what else might not be true: these are not minor adjustments. They are whole-life reckonings.

Late-discovery adoption is not a common specialty in Indiana. We name it explicitly because people searching for this support deserve to find a practice that already understands it.

How this page works

The record at the centre is one of five short exchanges. Each point on the ring is a lens, one way of reading it. Turn to a lens and the layer beneath the record rewrites. The record never does.

Teal marks in the record are the words the lens hangs on. Dotted clay underlines in the attribution are words the record never said. Hold two lenses to see two readings rest on the same words.

Your own sentence is the last point on the ring. Link each of your words to the word in the record it rests on; the page lists what is left unknown. Copy or download what you made. Start fresh clears what this browser saved.

    A concept from analytical psychology

    Projection

    In analytical psychology, projection names the act of attributing contents of one's own psyche to an outside object. The object is then experienced as holding what the person has not yet recognized as their own. Jung held that a projection needs a hook, some real feature of the object for it to hang on. The marked words in the record are the hook.

    Art therapists know the word from projective drawing tasks like House-Tree-Person. This page follows a different rule. The person who made the image says what it means.

    The concept describes a human mind. It does not decide what an AI system is, and neither does this page. The page shows how an interpretation moves while the evidence stays still.
